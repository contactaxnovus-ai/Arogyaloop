Design a high-fidelity, clickable prototype for ArogyaLoop by Axnovus, a hospital patient-journey platform for Indian hospitals. The purpose is to demonstrate how one patient moves from arrival at reception to 
follow-up after the visit.
Create a connected product experience, not a marketing website or a collection of unrelated dashboard screens. Every screen must use the same patient, visit ID, doctor, and care plan. Provide realistic sample 
data and make the main actions clickable so a presenter can walk through the journey in order.

Patient: Meera Sharma, age 46

Reason for visit: Persistent fatigue and dizziness

Assigned doctor: Dr Ananya Rao, General Medicine

Appointment: 10:30 AM today

Doctor’s sample plan: Complete blood count (CBC), follow-up consultation after the report, and a prescription sent to the hospital pharmacy

All information is fictional demo data. Do not present AI guidance as a medical diagnosis.
Create these connected screens and interactions:


Reception kiosk — patient arrival. A welcoming, touch-friendly screen with clear options for “I have an appointment,” “I need help finding a doctor,” and “Register a patient.” Show English and Hindi language 
choices, large text, and a simple accessibility-first layout. In the demo, Meera chooses help finding a doctor.
Guided intake and department routing. Ask a small number of plain-language questions about the reason for the visit. Show a recommendation for General Medicine, a short explanation in patient-friendly language, and 
an obvious option for reception staff to review or change the recommendation. Avoid diagnostic claims and emergency triage promises. Meera proceeds to Dr Ananya Rao.

Reception staff workspace. Show Meera’s arrival, registration details, appointment, assigned department, and current queue status in one compact view. Include a clear “Check in patient” action. After check-in, 
update the journey tracker and provide patient-facing directions to the correct department.

Doctor workspace. Show Meera’s relevant history, current reason for visit, consultation notes, and today’s visit on one screen. The doctor records the sample care plan and sends a CBC request to pathology and a 
prescription to pharmacy. Make the next steps visible, with human review before orders are confirmed.

Pathology workspace. Show the CBC request arriving with Meera’s visit ID. Demonstrate status changes from “Requested” to “Sample collected” to “Report ready.” Display a clearly labeled sample report, then show that 
the report is available in the doctor’s view and Meera’s patient timeline.

Pharmacy and payment workspace. Show the prescription associated with the same visit, a pharmacist review step, fulfilment status, and a payment summary. Use sample amounts in INR and label them as demo data. Do 
not imply automatic medicine substitution or dispensing without pharmacist review.

Patient view and follow-up. Show a simple visit summary with consultation, report, prescription, payment, and follow-up appointment. Include a sample reminder and patient communication screen. Show the follow-up 
as scheduled and the patient history updated with this visit.

Hospital operations overview. End with a compact view of the same patient journey: arrival → department guidance → consultation → pathology → pharmacy/payment → follow-up. Highlight completed steps, pending actions,
and who owns each next action. This is the closing screen for a hospital buyer presentation.Interaction and presentation requirements


Add a persistent, readable journey tracker across the staff-facing screens. Meera’s visit ID must remain consistent throughout.

Provide a “Next step” action on each screen so the full demonstration can be completed in 3–5 minutes.

Show distinct kiosk, reception, doctor, pathology, pharmacy, patient, and operations views, with appropriate information for each role.

Use realistic hospital interface details without cluttering the demo with unrelated HIS modules.

Design for an Indian hospital setting: familiar terminology, INR, mobile-friendly patient screens, and usable desktop staff screens.

Use a professional, calm visual style: deep teal or blue, warm neutral backgrounds, clear status colours, generous spacing, and strong typography.

Use the product name ArogyaLoop by Axnovus consistently.

Where useful, label the reception guidance as “AI-assisted, staff-reviewed.”

If mentioning ABDM, show only a discreet “Integration in progress” label. Do not show a completed government certification, live ABHA exchange, or any unsupported compliance badge.

The central message of the prototype should be: “Guide the patient from arrival to follow-up, with each care team seeing what needs to happen next.”
Generate all screens as one coherent prototype with clickable navigation and consistent sample data. Prioritise the patient journey and handoffs between teams over decorative charts.
